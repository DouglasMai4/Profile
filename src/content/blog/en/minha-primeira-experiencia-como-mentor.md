For some time, I've felt the desire to share some of what I've learned throughout my journey in tech. Not necessarily in a classroom or as a teacher, but by helping someone who is just getting started, sharing my experiences and knowledge, and hopefully making their journey a little easier than mine was.

When I started learning programming at the age of 10, most of what I learned came from free resources on the internet, especially YouTube. And I believe that, just like many developers, [Gustavo Guanabara](https://www.youtube.com/cursoemvideo) was one of my first major references. (I think almost every Brazilian developer has a story involving him, haha!)

Over the years, I started thinking that I wanted to give something back. That's why I reached out to [Escola da Nuvem](https://escoladanuvem.org), looking for an opportunity to volunteer as a mentor.

My goal was to help someone who was just starting their journey in tech, but I also saw it as an opportunity to grow, both professionally and personally.

And what an experience it was!

## Getting Started: Anxiety and Expectations

I have to admit, I was quite anxious before our first meeting. I was a little nervous about how things would go, but at the same time, I was really excited.

Our first meeting was mostly a traditional introduction. We introduced ourselves, and I wanted to learn more about my mentee, her background, her interests, and most importantly, where she was in her career journey.

She already knew she wanted to work in tech, but she wasn't sure which area to pursue.

That's when I started thinking about how to approach our mentoring sessions.

I didn't want to simply point her toward a specific field and tell her that it was the best path to follow. After all, I believe she's the person best suited to make that decision. My role was to help her understand herself better, explore possibilities, and find her own answers.

In the following sessions, I introduced some exercises to help her with that process of self-discovery. One of them was particularly interesting: at one point, she couldn't identify a pattern in her answers, but I managed to notice a connection that had gone unnoticed.

When I shared my perspective, she thought it was really cool.

Little by little, we made progress. During our final sessions, our conversations became more natural because she was getting to know herself better and had a clearer idea of what she wanted to pursue.

## Discovering That I Enjoy Teaching

One of the best things about this experience was realizing that I wasn't just helping someone else find their path. I was discovering something about myself too.

I genuinely enjoy teaching!

It was also impossible not to see a bit of myself in someone who was just starting out. It reminded me of my own doubts, discoveries, and everything I had to learn throughout my journey.

But it wasn't all about sharing knowledge.

At some point, my mentee asked me some technical questions that I simply didn't know how to answer. And that's okay! Those moments pushed me to research, study, and look for answers I didn't have yet.

It was a reminder that even after several years working in tech, there's still so much to learn.

I've always believed that teaching is also a way of learning. This experience gave me the chance to see that firsthand.

## The Final Session and What I Took Away

We eventually reached our final meeting, and we had a really nice conversation about the entire experience.

She shared some very positive feedback about the mentoring process and talked about how she had noticed her own progress along the way.

We discussed the journey, what had changed, and at one point, she asked me why I had decided to become a mentor.

It was interesting to realize that something that started simply as a way to give back had turned into such a meaningful experience for me as well.

If I felt anxious during our first meeting, by the last one I was much more confident. I had learned how to guide our conversations in a more natural and effective way.

And I left this experience with one thing in mind: I want to keep mentoring others.

## If You've Ever Wanted to Become a Mentor, Just Start!

If there's one thing I'd like to share with others after this experience, it's this: don't wait until you feel completely ready to start.

You might not know how to answer every question. Your first sessions might not go exactly as planned. You might need to research, study, and rethink some of your own ideas along the way.

And that's perfectly fine!

You don't need to know everything to help someone who's just getting started. You just need to be willing to share what you know, acknowledge what you don't know yet, and learn alongside them.

In the end, I believe you'll always leave the experience a little better than when you started. That's exactly what happened to me.

I started out wanting to share my experiences and knowledge, but ended up discovering something new that I genuinely enjoy, becoming more confident, and finding even more motivation to grow as a professional and as a person.

Sometimes, while helping someone find their own path, we end up discovering a little more about our own.

And that was, without a doubt, one of the best parts of my first mentoring experience.
